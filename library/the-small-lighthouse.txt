Once there was a lighthouse so small that the big ships never noticed it.

Every night it switched on its little lamp anyway. It did not shine far, only to the end of the old wooden pier, but that was exactly where the fishing boats came home.

One foggy evening the great lighthouse on the hill went dark. The big ships slowed down and waited. The fishing boats, however, came home as always, following the small, steady light at the end of the pier.

In the morning the fishermen painted the small lighthouse a fresh shade of blue. It did not grow any taller. It did not need to. It had learned that a light does not have to reach everyone. It only has to reach someone.
