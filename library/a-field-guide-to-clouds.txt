Clouds are made of tiny water droplets or ice crystals floating in the air. They form when moist air rises, cools, and the water vapour in it condenses.

Cumulus clouds are the puffy, cotton-like clouds with flat bottoms. On a sunny afternoon they usually mean fair weather.

Stratus clouds are flat, grey layers that can cover the whole sky like a blanket. They often bring drizzle or mist.

Cirrus clouds are thin and wispy and float very high up. They are made of ice crystals and can look like brush strokes across the blue.

Cumulonimbus clouds are the giants. They can tower many kilometres into the sky and bring thunder, lightning and heavy rain.

A good way to learn clouds is simply to look up every day and give each one a name.
