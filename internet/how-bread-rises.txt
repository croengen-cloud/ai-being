Bread dough rises because of yeast, a tiny living fungus.

When yeast is mixed with flour and water, it feeds on the sugars in the dough. As it feeds, it releases carbon dioxide gas.

Kneading the dough builds gluten, a stretchy network of proteins. The gluten traps the gas bubbles, so the dough puffs up like a balloon.

In the oven the heat makes the gas expand even more, then the yeast dies and the structure sets. That is why a good loaf is full of little holes.

Top tip from the comments: be patient. Dough rises faster in a warm kitchen.
